Ozone Pollution in Chicago

Air pollution adversely impacts human health and is a leading cause of premature mortality worldwide (Cohen et al., 2017). Tropospheric ozone pollution is a concern across major cities, including Chicago. While the United States Environmental Protection Agency sets national air quality standards for ozone, many cities remain in nonattainment status, meaning that air quality monitoring stations record concentrations at levels harmful to human and environmental health. According to these national standards, Chicago has been in a severe nonattainment status since January of 2025 (EPA, 2026).

Tropospheric ozone forms from interactions between gaseous pollutants, including nitrogen oxide (NOx) and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) (Nawaz et al., 2025). NOx refers to both nitrogen oxide and nitrogen dioxide (NO2). NO2 is the more stable species of NOx and is directly regulated under national air quality standards, making NO2 a practical proxy for understanding the role of NOx in ozone production (Dickens, 2023).

Data Products

This dashboard integrates three complementary data products to provide a comprehensive view of air quality conditions over the Chicago region. Each product offers unique spatial coverage, temporal resolution, and measurement capabilities that, when combined, enable a more complete understanding of ozone precursor distributions and their evolution.

NASA TEMPO (Tropospheric Emissions: Monitoring of Pollution)

NASA's Tropospheric Emissions: Monitoring of Pollution (TEMPO) instrument is a revolutionary geostationary satellite mission that was launched in April of 2023. TEMPO measures key gases and aerosols that impact air quality, including ozone and NO2, across North America. As a geostationary satellite with a fixed position above North America, TEMPO scans the concentrations of air pollutants hourly. This project used the Level 3 Gridded NO2 Tropospheric and Stratospheric Columns Version 04 product for the analysis, which was released in September of 2025.

Image Credit: NASA

OpenAir Chicago (CLARITY Sensor Network)

OpenAir Chicago, part of the Community Logic for Air Quality (CLARITY) initiative, is a dense, community-based air quality monitoring network that launched in September of 2025 (Green, 2026). With 277 of Clarity's low-cost, Node-S sensors deployed in a grid-based approach across Chicago, Open Air Chicago provides sub-hourly readings of NO2 and PM2.5 concentrations. All sensors in Chicago are located less than a mile from each other.

Fusion Layer (Satellite + Ground Data Integration)

The fused data layer provides estimates of ground-level NO2 concentrations using TEMPO's NO2 tropospheric column retrievals. With this data layer, users are able to estimate NO2 concentrations across Chicago and the broader Cook County, even if Open Air Chicago sensors go offline.
