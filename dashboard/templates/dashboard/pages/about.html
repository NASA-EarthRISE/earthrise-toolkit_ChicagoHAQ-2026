NASA EarthRISE Developers Academy

NASA EarthRISE Developers Academy builds the next generation of science and technology leaders by immersing student and early career scientists in projects using NASA data to address real-world challenges. EarthRISE Developers Academy hosts 10 to 14-week projects center on developing innovative applied science solutions to help partners make more effective, efficient decisions. Through collaborative projects, professional development, and cooperation with state, local, tribal, territorial, and private sector partners, the Academy equips participants with skills to accelerate innovation and bridge Earth science with the future of space exploration.

Project Overview

Tropospheric ozone is a major public health concern in the Chicago metropolitan area, with nitrogen dioxide (NO2) serving as a practical proxy for understanding spatiotemporal patterns of pollution. The Chicago Health & Air Quality research team worked with partners from the Lake Michigan Air Directors Consortium (LADCO) on a project that aimed to assess regional air quality concerns from potential sources of NO2 pollution across Chicago and the broader Cook County, Illinois, area. Utilizing TEMPO satellite data and Open Air Chicago ground sensor data collected from September 2025 to June 2026, the team created a fused data product that estimates surface-level NO2 concentrations using a collection of data sources and estimating parameters. This dashboard visualizes those spatiotemporal trends of NO2 pollution across Chicago and its community areas.

Meet the Partners

The Lake Michigan Air Directors Consortium (LADCO) is a multi-jurisdictional research and planning organization that provides technical expertise to state, local, and tribal agencies in the Great Lakes Region to aid air pollution mitigation efforts and maintain regulatory standards outlined by the Clean Air Act. LADCO is a convener of resources, information, and expertise on air pollution matters in the region and facilitates training for member states to discuss technical issues related to air quality planning. LADCO currently uses data from surface air monitors, satellites, and computer models to identify emission sources and quantify exposure in the Great Lakes region. Since ground-level monitoring is sparse in many areas, or unable to capture cohesive trends, LADCO's modeling abilities are essential to expanding fine-scale and transboundary air pollution databases within the Great Lakes Region by helping member states use the models to explore air pollution control programs and achieve their clean air goals. The EarthRISE Developers Academy would like to thank Zac Adelman, Angie Dickens, and Grant Coleman from LADCO for their valuable insights over the course of their project.
